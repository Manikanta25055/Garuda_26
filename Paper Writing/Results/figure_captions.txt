IEEE Paper — Figure Result Descriptions
========================================

Fig. 1 — Training Loss Convergence (Box, Classification, DFL) — v5 Final Model, 150 Epochs
All three loss components decrease steadily over 150 epochs, with box loss
dropping from 1.65 to 0.74 and classification loss from 2.80 to 0.49,
confirming stable convergence without divergence. The close tracking between
train and validation curves indicates the model generalises well and is not
overfitting to the training distribution.

Fig. 2 — Validation mAP and Precision/Recall vs Epoch — v5 Final Model
mAP@0.5 rises sharply in the first 30 epochs, then plateaus around 0.81 by
epoch 100, with mAP@0.5:0.95 following at approximately 0.62, showing the
model learns coarse localisation first before refining tighter box boundaries.
Precision and recall converge near 0.87 and 0.80 respectively,
indicating a well-balanced detector with no strong bias toward false positives
or false negatives.

Fig. 3 — Four-Model Performance Comparison (Test Set): Default → v1 → v2 → v5
The default COCO-pretrained YOLOv8s scores near zero on all metrics because
its 80-class label scheme does not align with the 4-class task, confirming
that out-of-the-box weights are completely unusable for this domain. The final
fine-tuned v5 model (4,982 training images, 150 epochs) achieves mAP@0.5 of
0.847 — an 82% improvement over fine-tuned v1 and a 12% gain over v2 —
demonstrating that both dataset scale and diversity are critical for robust
real-world detection performance.

Fig. 4 — Per-Class mAP@0.5: Fine-tuned v1 vs v2 vs v5
The largest individual gain from v1 to v5 belongs to Knife (+0.66 mAP@0.5),
which was severely underperforming in v1 (0.23) due to the Roboflow dataset
containing very few diverse knife images. Scissors achieves the highest
absolute mAP@0.5 of 0.967 in v5, while Person remains the most challenging
class at 0.647, reflecting high intra-class visual variability across poses,
occlusions, and backgrounds. All four classes improve monotonically from v1
through v2 to v5.

Fig. 5 — Full Per-Class Metrics for Best Model — Fine-tuned v5 (Final)
Hammer, Knife, and Scissors achieve strong balanced performance with both
precision and recall above 0.82, while Person is the weakest class with
precision 0.701 and recall 0.609, reflecting the high intra-class visual
variability of persons across poses, occlusions, and backgrounds. Scissors
achieves exceptional mAP@0.5 of 0.967 and mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.828, making it
the best-detected class overall.

Fig. 6 — Normalised Confusion Matrix (Fine-tuned v5, Test Set)
The diagonal dominates with correct-class recall values of 0.85, 0.82, 0.61,
and 0.93 for Hammer, Knife, Person, and Scissors respectively, confirming
strong per-class discrimination. Inter-class confusion is very low (≤0.03
per cell), indicating that most detection errors are outright misses
(background false negatives) rather than misclassifications between the four
target classes.

Fig. 7 — Precision-Recall Curves (Fine-tuned v5, Per Class)
Scissors achieves the highest area under the PR curve (AP=0.967), maintaining
high precision well past recall of 0.90, while Person shows the steepest
precision drop-off at lower recall thresholds, consistent with its high
intra-class variability. Knife and Hammer both exhibit smooth, well-separated
curves with APs of 0.886 and 0.889 respectively, demonstrating reliable
detection across a wide confidence operating range.

Fig. 8 — Dataset Class Distribution (Train / Val / Test) — v5 Dataset
Person is the most represented class with 2,100 training instances, while
Hammer is the scarcest with 480, reflecting the natural frequency imbalance
in the source datasets. This imbalance partially explains Person's lower
precision (0.701) — abundant training examples introduce more background
diversity and harder negatives — while the full 4,982-image training set
allows v5 to substantially outperform earlier checkpoints on all classes.

Fig. 9 — Hardware Inference Performance on Hailo-8L
Our custom model (best_v5.hef) achieves 52.2 FPS at 18.4 ms NPU latency on
the Hailo-8L chip, comfortably exceeding real-time requirements (≥25 FPS) for
security surveillance applications. This compares favourably in latency against
the standard YOLOv8s reference (13.2 ms / 58.2 FPS), with the modest 5 ms
increase attributed to the custom NMS post-processing baked into the HEF.

Fig. 10 — F1-Confidence Threshold Curves (Fine-tuned v5, Per Class)
All four classes reach their peak F1 score in the confidence range 0.25–0.27,
which aligns with YOLO's default threshold of 0.25 and confirms that no manual
threshold tuning is required for deployment. Scissors achieves the highest
peak F1 (0.690), while Person is lowest (0.473), consistent with its lower
recall; operating below the peak threshold for Person could recover additional
true positives at a modest false-positive cost.

Fig. 11 — Validation mAP@0.5: Effect of Training Dataset Size (v1 vs v2 vs v5)
Fine-tuned v1 (359 images) converges quickly to a high validation mAP of
~0.88 but its held-out test mAP is only 0.465, exposing severe overfitting to
the Roboflow dataset's visual style. Fine-tuned v2 (1,383 images) narrows the
gap substantially (val 0.735, test 0.754), while the final v5 (4,982 images,
150 epochs) achieves both strong validation (0.817) and best test mAP (0.847),
demonstrating that dataset scale and diversity are the dominant factors for
real-world generalisation.

Fig. 12 — Val vs Test mAP@0.5 Generalisation Gap (v1, v2, v5)
Fine-tuned v1 exhibits a 0.414-point gap between its best validation mAP
(0.879) and held-out test mAP (0.465), a clear sign of distribution overfit
to the single-source Roboflow dataset. Fine-tuned v2 reverses this gap
slightly (+0.019, test marginally beats val), and the final v5 model achieves
positive generalisation (+0.030 gap: test 0.847 > val 0.817), confirming that
the expanded 4,982-image dataset produces a model that generalises robustly
to genuinely unseen real-world imagery.
